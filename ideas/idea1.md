## Problem / scarcity
Capital misallocates because forecasts treat industries independently while
research automation creates cross-sector feedback loops; meanwhile AI abundance
induces demand for physical validation, permissions, and verification faster
than supply responds. Cost of being wrong: crowded consensus trades + missed
bottleneck migration.

exposure scoring → three-clock event dating → surprise-weighted ranking →
belief-consistency arbitrage across markets. Feedify as sensory nervous system;

## Thesis (verbatim, formatting lightly normalized)

The kernel is still one abstraction too shallow. The useful object is not merely
future world − current world. It is: **what does increasing abundance make newly
scarce?** AGI makes cognition, search, design, coding, simulation and hypothesis
generation abundant. That abundance creates induced demand for their complements.
Those complements become bottlenecks, attract rents, trigger investment, get
relieved, and push scarcity elsewhere. Model the moving scarcity surface.

### 1. Invest in scarcity migration
INTELLIGENCE ABUNDANT → hypotheses/designs/code abundant → VERIFICATION scarce →
automated → PHYSICAL EXPERIMENTS scarce → scaled → INSTRUMENTS/MATERIALS scarce →
manufacturing scales → ENERGY/GRID/PERMISSIONS scarce → … → new bottleneck.
Research automation in one sector raises research productivity elsewhere (feedback
loops); progress cannot be represented as independent industry forecasts. Graph
edges must be catalytic: A raises the rate at which B itself improves.

### 2. Jevons paradox for science (top alpha)
AI cutting hypothesis cost 10,000× can make physical validation MORE valuable
(million things worth testing). Cheap theorems → scarce verification; cheap chip
design → scarce tape-outs; cheap materials → scarce synthesis; cheap proteins →
scarce assays; cheap robot policies → scarce robot-hours. d(demand for physical
validation)/d(AI capability) > 0, possibly strongly. Ask not which drug AI
discovers but what apparatus all 50 candidates must pass through.

### 3. Cross-world monopolies
Score businesses by cross-world necessity: sum over scenarios of P(s) × needed(s).
Prefer suppliers necessary across many mutually exclusive futures (measurement,
control, cryogenics, photonics validation) over picking architecture winners.

### 4. Ranked alpha extensions (effort: 80% on first eight)
1 experimental scarcity, 2 permission scarcity, 3 bottleneck migration velocity
(dB/dt, not B), 4 technical-half-life mismatch, 5 cross-world necessities,
6 real AI usage telemetry, 7 three-clock arbitrage (capability/deployment/cashflow),
8 verification economy, 9 negative-data moat, 10 research-automation multiplier,
11 market reaction graph, 12 benchmark-to-reality gap, 13 idea-space expansion,
14 threshold cliffs, 15 capital-cycle inversion, 16 geographic constraints,
17 AI-generated cannibalization, 18 supply-chain substitutability.

### 5. Permission scarcity (possibly more underpriced than physical)
2,500 GW in global grid queues; data-centre build 1–3 yrs vs grids 5–15 yrs; ~20%
of planned DC capacity delayed to 2030; transformers 4 yrs, DC cables 5+ yrs.
Measure Deliverable MW = announced × P(site) × P(interconnect) × P(transformer)
× P(generation) × P(permit). Generalizes: FDA validation, licensed sites,
certified deployment, fab allocation, safety certification (UL 3115, Mar 2026).

### 6. Scarcity vs acceleration; entry AND exit
Track dB/dt and d²B/dt² (lead times, utilization, vacancies, used prices, expedite
fees, dual-sourcing). Bottleneck alpha = identify bottleneck − identify supply
response (capacity, lead-time collapse). Bottleneck derivative: what gets
constrained IF the current constraint scales (metrology on HBM success; test on
CPO volume). LPKF shape: small cashflows + potential critical node.

### 7. AI-to-atoms interface (organizing worldview)
DIGITAL (models/reasoning/simulation) → INTERFACE (measurement, verification,
robotics, instrumentation, metrology) → PHYSICAL (atoms/cells/energy/materials).
AGI zeroes the top layer fastest; the interface layer (bits↔atoms both directions)
becomes extraordinarily important. Biology version: value moves to testing,
growing, measuring, manufacturing living systems reproducibly (liquid handling,
mass spec, sequencing, assays, bioreactors, lab robotics).

### 8. Telemetry + clocks + reflexivity
Real usage (380T OpenRouter tokens: AI Premium) beats model priors for exposure;
ground in evidence (platform choice alone swings estimates). Three clocks per
event (capability/deployment/cashflow); markets are causal (reflexivity:
prices → capex → world). Actor/reaction graph: predict who must react next
(71% of fund-manager trades predictable from prior behavior).

### 9. News, benchmarks, moats
Surprise(e) = e − E[e|W]; weight by credibility, not impressiveness. Benchmarks
increasingly mineable → Economic Transfer Score hierarchy (verified cash-flow
impact highest). Negative experimental data + irreducibility (cost genuine
observation − cost synthetic approximation) as moats. Technical half-life vs
valuation duration → obsolescence shorts; revenue-per-friction businesses as
shorts; output-priced scarcity (MW tested, assays run) as longs.

### 10. Master equation
Alpha = Σ (P_ours − P_market) × ΔCF × cross-world exposure × scarcity(B, dB/dt)
× irreducibility − crowdedness, with B endogenous
(induced demand × indispensability × replacement time × permission friction /
capacity + substitutes + inventory).

names), world-state consistency arbitrage (reverse-DCF thousands; find mutually
inconsistent implied futures), real-usage destruction graph, half-life shorts.
Feedify as sensory nervous system: bottleneck/capability/obsolescence/worldstate
