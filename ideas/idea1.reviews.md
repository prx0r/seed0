# idea1 peer review R1 (seed0 reviewer, 2026-09-10) — hostile read, scored

## Verdict: strong research program, not yet a model. Fund the slices, not the whole.
The core (scarcity migration + Jevons-for-science + permission scarcity) survives.
The master equation does not — yet. Details below, strongest objections first.

## Objection 1 (serious): the master equation is numerology, not math
Alpha = Σ belief_gap × ΔCF × X × B × R − C mixes probability gaps, pounds,
unitless scores, and a subtracted crowdedness term with incompatible units and
no weights, normalization, or calibration procedure. As written it cannot be
computed twice the same way. Counter: treat it as a scoring checklist (which is
what criteria1 enforces), and require each term's 0–1 scaling + weight to be
committed BEFORE scoring any company. Until then, quote it as doctrine, never
as output.

## Objection 2 (serious): Jevons vs permission — internal tension unresolved
The Jevons argument (more candidates → more validation demand) is strongest
exactly where §5 says permission is fixed (FDA throughput, grid queues). If the
regulator can't scale, induced demand becomes deadweight, not revenue — the
bottleneck earns rents only if SOMEONE can sell into it. The thesis needs, per
domain, a ruling: does induced demand clear through prices (bullish owners) or
die in queues (bullish nobody)? Currently asserts both sides.

## Objection 3 (serious): cross-world P(s) repeats the sin §16 condemns
§16 rightly demands evidence-grounded exposure over model priors — then §3
scores companies on scenario probabilities with no sourcing procedure. Who sets
P(superconducting wins)? Unfixed, cross-world exposure is priors all the way
down. Fix: probabilities must cite a market (prediction markets, capex revealed
preference) or be flagged uniform-with-wide-bands.

## Objection 4 (moderate): reflexivity eats the arbitrage
If managers learn from prices (§18) and fund flows are predictable (§19), the
belief-inconsistency trade (§37) has a half-life: the marginal loser must be
named (mandate-constrained holders? passive flows? slow boards?). Without a
loser story, assume 6–18 month decay and size accordingly.

## Objection 5 (moderate): duration mismatch needs a base rate
Accounting lives (e.g., 5–6 yr DC equipment) may already embed obsolescence —
hyperscalers EXTENDED lives recently, signaling confidence, not denial. The
short engine needs evidence that markets overestimate duration in the specific
name, not the general claim that tech moves fast.

## Objection 6 (minor but load-bearing): no discounting, no horizon
A 2035 bottleneck payoff at 2026 rates is worth a fraction of par. Add time and
discount to every slice or the ranking implicitly favors far-future stories.

## Objection 7 (minor): dB/dt data availability
Lead times, utilization, expedite fees for 100–200 obscure names are private,
lagging, and gameable. The fingerprint list is excellent; the collection plan
doesn't exist. This is the real work — budget it before the math.

## What survives untouched
Three clocks, surprise() primitive, evidence hierarchy/Economic Transfer Score,
crowdedness as a term, threshold cliffs as cost curves, the interface-layer
framing. All implementable, all in criteria1.

## Recommendation (unchanged, strengthened)
Fund ONE slice first: grid-permission Deliverable MW — most measurable, most
falsifiable, nearest revenue. Demand a worked numeric example before expanding
to biology/quantum. Score this review against criteria1-C12's demo when built.
